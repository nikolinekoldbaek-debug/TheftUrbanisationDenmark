# Theft and urbanisation in Denmark 1866-1906

Hypothesis: Theft increased as Denmark became more urbanised during industrialisation.

Author: Nikoline Koldbæk Kaiser

Resume: This project investigates whether theft increased as Denmark became more urbanised during industrialisation between 1866–1870 and 1901–1905. Using digitised criminal statistics from Danmarks Statistik, I analysed theft convictions in relation to urban growth, population change, and industrialisation. The project combines digital methods, data cleaning, and historical interpretation to compare both raw theft totals and population-adjusted rates. The results show that theft convictions increased in absolute numbers and that urban areas were over-represented in theft statistics, especially in the earlier period. However, when adjusted for population growth, theft convictions per 100,000 inhabitants actually declined. The project therefore concludes that industrialisation and urbanisation changed the geography and visibility of theft more than they increased theft relative to population size.
